# What do Meta's rules allow for a self-serve listing assistant?

Type: research
Status: resolved
Blocked by:

## Question

Under model B, the Seller posts their own Listing, but Clay's agent still drafts it, writes buyer replies, and reads Facebook Marketplace asking prices as comps. Against Meta's own text (Terms of Service, Commerce Policies, Marketplace rules, automated data collection terms), what is allowed and what isn't for:

1. Looking up Marketplace Listings as price comps on behalf of paying Sellers (by hand in a signed-in browser, or automated).
2. A Seller posting a Listing and replies written by AI for a fee. Are there disclosure rules?
3. Anything in the Commerce Policies that changes which Items the service should refuse.

Cite each answer to the Meta page it comes from.

## Answer

Findings: [research/01-meta-terms-for-self-serve.md](../research/01-meta-terms-for-self-serve.md) (also on branch `research/meta-terms`). **Not yet verified:** facebook.com was blocked from the research session, so the quotes come from search excerpts of Meta's own pages. The file lists 7 pages to open and check; see [Check the Meta findings against Meta's own pages](11-verify-meta-findings.md).

The scope widened mid-research to include posting for the Seller.

1. **Comps:** an agent reading Marketplace automatically breaks the Terms (no automated data collection without written permission), and so does selling data taken from Meta. Looking by hand and writing a price range in Clay's own words, or the Seller using Meta's own price suggestion, is fine.
2. **AI-written Listings and replies:** allowed. Meta's own tools draft listings and auto-reply, and its AI-disclosure rules cover realistic video and audio, not text. The rule that applies is "not misleading".
3. **Refuse list:** recalled or banned products, animals, health and ingestible items, hazardous materials, weapons, alcohol and tobacco, adult items, counterfeits, digital goods, gift cards and tickets, services and rentals, and anything the Seller doesn't own (the list is partial).
4. **Posting on a Seller's account:** not allowed. Sharing logins or sessions breaks the Terms, and an AI driving the Seller's browser counts as automated access.
5. **Approved routes for third parties:** none found for individuals selling used Items.
6. **Listing from Clay's own profile:** conflicts with personal-use, person-to-person terms.

Side findings: Clay's current setup (Claude in Chrome posting and reading Marketplace on his own profile) carries the same automated-access risk. Also, the service can't be advertised on Marketplace, because services aren't allowed there.
